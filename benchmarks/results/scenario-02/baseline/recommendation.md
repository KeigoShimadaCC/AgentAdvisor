## Executive recommendation
Recommend **not** investing the full $25,000 now.  
Best choice: **invest $10,000 now on a standard post-money SAFE, and make the remaining $15,000 contingent on both a signed pilot and a working MVP within the next six months**. If your friend resists objective milestones, formal documents, or basic investor rights, **decline for now and revisit after traction data exists**. This best balances upside, discipline, and friendship protection.

## Decision confidence
**Recommendation confidence: medium.** The logic is strong because this is a single, concentrated angel bet, where variance is extreme and base rates are harsh [1][2][3].  
**Evidence confidence: medium-low.** I have base-rate and financing evidence, but no company-specific diligence on team quality, customer pull, burn, or product velocity.  
**Major uncertainty:** whether this founder can reach real paid usage quickly. At pre-seed, that matters far more than small differences in cap.

## Alternatives considered
1. **Stage $10,000 now, $15,000 after signed pilot + MVP**  
   Best overall. It preserves the relationship signal, buys more information, and caps your immediate risk.

2. **Decline now and revisit in six months**  
   Better than investing the full amount now if $10,000 is still emotionally or financially meaningful, or if the founder will not accept clear milestones. It is slightly worse than staging because you lose optionality and may miss supporting the company at the highest-upside moment.

3. **Invest the full $25,000 now**  
   Lowest-ranked. It maximizes exposure before the two most important de-risking events, product proof and customer proof, have happened.

## Key reasons
- **This is a single, concentrated startup bet, not a diversified angel portfolio.** Early-stage returns are power-law driven, and portfolio breadth matters a lot [2][3].
- **Pre-seed failure risk is high.** Running out of capital, weak product-market fit, timing, and unit economics dominate failure patterns [1].
- **Staging creates option value.** You preserve 60% of the contemplated capital until real evidence arrives.
- **Friendship risk rises with ambiguity.** A smaller first check with explicit milestones is less likely to create later resentment than either a vague promise or a full emotional check.

## Scenario analysis
These are rough base-rate ranges, not precise company-specific forecasts.

- **Upside, 8% to 12%:** MVP works, pilot converts into real customer pull, a credible seed round happens, and the company eventually reaches a venture-scale outcome. This is the path where the investment earns strong multiples [2].
- **Base, 20% to 25%:** product gets built and maybe piloted, but commercial repeatability stays weak. Outcomes include a long slog, flat follow-on round, acquihire, or a modest exit, often producing poor to middling returns.
- **Downside, 60% to 70%:** no durable product-market fit or no financing escape velocity, so the company stalls and most or all invested capital is lost [1].
- **Tail risk, separate and non-additive, 5% to 10%:** the friendship gets strained by unclear expectations, follow-on pressure, or disappointment, even if the company does not formally fail.

## Quantitative findings
- At a **$6M post-money SAFE cap**, a **$25,000** check buys about **0.4167%** ownership before future dilution. A **$10,000** check buys about **0.1667%** [4].
- After typical later-round dilution of roughly **40% to 60%**, the **$25,000** stake may end up around **0.17% to 0.25%** at exit.
- At that diluted ownership, the company roughly needs to exit at about:
  - **$10M to $15M** for you to just get your money back
  - **$30M to $45M** for about **3x**
  - **$100M to $150M** for about **10x**
  - **$200M to $300M** for about **20x**
- So the economics only become compelling if you believe there is a real chance of a **large** outcome, not just a decent small business outcome.
- Staging changes the risk math materially: if milestones are missed, you lose at most **$10,000**, not **$25,000**, while preserving the right to invest more if evidence improves.

## Strongest counterarguments
- **“The best returns come from investing before traction.”**  
  True in some winners, but that logic works better inside a broad portfolio than in one emotionally complicated friend investment [2][3].

- **“A $6M cap could be attractive for an AI startup.”**  
  Possibly. But at this stage, execution and customer evidence dominate valuation finesse. A fair cap does not rescue a weak company.

- **“A partial check may offend the founder.”**  
  Maybe. But a clear, principled milestone structure is usually healthier than a full check you later regret.

## Critical assumptions
- Losing **$25,000**, or even **$10,000**, is meaningful enough that regret would matter.
- You do **not** have unique founder or market information that meaningfully improves the base rates.
- The company can define objective six-month milestones, especially a working MVP and a signed pilot.
- The investment will use **standard documents**, ideally a post-money SAFE with basic information rights and, if possible, pro rata rights [4].
- Protecting the friendship is a real objective, not just a nice-to-have.

## What would change the recommendation
I would move closer to **invest the full $25,000 now** if most of the following became true:
- a **signed paid pilot** exists,
- there is a **working MVP** with credible user engagement,
- a strong **lead investor** is entering,
- the founder shows exceptional **founder-market fit** and speed,
- the cap table and SAFE terms are clean,
- and you would still feel fine if the whole amount went to zero.

I would move to **decline now** if any of the following happen:
- the founder resists milestone-based staging,
- the documents are informal or unclear,
- the pilot is only verbal,
- the MVP is mostly roadmap, not product,
- or you suspect that a loss would damage the friendship.

## Next actions
1. Decide your real loss limit: ask yourself whether losing **$10,000** would still feel acceptable and non-resentful.
2. Tell your friend you want to separate friendship from underwriting, and offer **$10,000 now plus $15,000 after signed pilot + working MVP**.
3. Request a compact diligence pack: SAFE, cap table, current investors, burn/runway, product demo, pilot terms, and target milestones.
4. Require **objective milestone definitions** now, not later.
5. If milestones are met, reassess the second tranche using customer evidence, not optimism.
6. If the structure creates tension, politely decline and revisit in six months.
